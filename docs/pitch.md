# ezworkspace: pitch package

## 1. One-liner and taglines

**Headline promise: No Worker Worse Off.** Falling behind on AI is no longer a reason to let someone go, because catching up is built into the job.

**One-liner:** ezworkspace brings existing workers and new hires into AI-era work. They text or talk to a private AI coach. Their COO sees the team's AI map only after signing No Worker Worse Off. It's cheaper and faster than firing, re-hiring or courses.

1. Better off, not laid off.
2. Keep your people. Move them into AI-era work.
3. Cheaper than replacing. Faster than a course.

## 4. Ten hardest questions

1. **How big is this?** Every back-office worker in a non-tech industry is a seat. The median company already spends about $11–12 per employee per month on AI ([BERI](https://www.beri.net/article/ai-spending-7500-per-employee-ramp-index-2026)).
2. **Why now?** 57% hide their AI use; only 40% have an AI policy ([HRGrapevine](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-05-02-covert-use-of-artificial-intelligence-at-work-poses-risk-study-finds)). Workday entered the category Oct 13 ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-13-Workday-Introduces-Work-Intelligence-to-Help-Customers-Design-Work-for-AI)); Colorado's AI disclosure law starts Jan 1, 2027 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/payroll/overhaul-of-colorado-ai-bias-law-headed-to-polis-for-signature)).
3. **Moat vs Reejig and Workday?** They sell to employers, so workers hide from them; they can't credibly seal data from their own customer. Reejig fits 10,000+ employee firms ([RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/enterprise-software-enterprise-application-software-eas-enterprise-service-management-esm/talent-intelligence-platforms/reejig)); Workday's is early access for its HCM customers ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-13-Workday-Introduces-Work-Intelligence-to-Help-Customers-Design-Work-for-AI)).
4. **Why would a COO pay for something workers control?** The alternatives cost more. Replacing one AP clerk: $24,605–$98,420, half to twice the $49,210 BLS clerk median ([Gallup](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/247391/fixable-problem-costs-businesses-trillion.aspx), [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/bookkeeping-accounting-and-auditing-clerks.htm)). A bootcamp: ~$14,000 and ~14 weeks per person, workflow unchanged ([Course Report](https://www.coursereport.com/coding-bootcamp-ultimate-guide)). We move people into the new work inside the job, and the map shows where.
5. **Isn't this a nicer layoff tool?** No map until the employer signs No Worker Worse Off. Team totals only; AI readiness shows counts, never names. Firecrawl flags any signer's WARN notice for human review. We charge per employee, so layoffs cut our revenue.
6. **Unit economics?** Pricing isn't tonight's focus. The hypothesis is a per-employee seat inside HR AI's $5–15 per employee per month ([Workativ](https://workativ.com/hr/blog/hr-chatbot-pricing)), so a department pilot stays under the $50–100K proof-of-concept benchmark ([coworker.ai](https://coworker.ai/blog/how-much-should-enterprise-ai-cost-per-user)).
7. **What if workers lie?** Self-reports only draw the map. Nobody earns money from them, and amnesty makes the truth safe, so there's little reason to inflate. Pilots check the map against measured team cycle time. Tonight's demo uses reported minutes.
8. **Is the Compact enforceable?** A pilot-contract clause and a public badge the company can lose. Firecrawl checks signers for layoff notices. Union AI contracts already carry no-layoff terms ([metaintro](https://www.metaintro.com/blog/unions-ai-protections-2026-contracts), a weaker source). Counsel reviews it before any paid pilot.
9. **Go-to-market?** Top-down: a COO with a productivity target buys a pilot in AP, where cycle time is measurable. Then bottom-up: workers recruit teammates to reach quorum, and shortcuts spread as playbooks. iMessage and web chat today; SMS and phone calls next.
10. **Why you?** I'm in SJSU's Responsible Computing Club; making AI adoption fair to workers is the problem I chose. I built this loop tonight and talked to [N] workers doing it.

## 6. Demo-failure playbook

**Prep:** `docs/demo-runbook.md` (T-30 checklist, between-judge reset), plus a second browser profile signed in as COO.

**If iMessage lags:** Say "iMessage is taking a second"; at 5/5 the text landed, so sign anyway. After 20 seconds: "Same coach, on the web." Open `/#/text`, tap the mic and let the judge say the task; the reply is read aloud. No mic? Type it. From the laptop instead, in `web/` with `AGENT_SECRET` exported:

```sh
npx convex run ingest:text "{\"secret\":\"$AGENT_SECRET\",\"from\":\"+1YOURCELL\",\"body\":\"ChatGPT drafts my vendor emails, saves me 2 hours a week\"}"
```

Rerun with `PLAN` as the body. Relay crashed? `bun start` in `agent/`; queued texts still go out.

**If a judge sends a voice memo or asks to call:** The agent asks them to type. Calls need Photon's Business plan, so they're off tonight; offer the web chat's mic instead.

**If Wi-Fi drops:** Laptop to the iPhone hotspot; no signal, play the backup video, then Q&A.

**If sign-in fails:** Use the second profile. If Clerk is down, `npx convex run team:signCompact --identity '{"name":"COO, Acme Insurance","subject":"coo","issuer":"cli"}'` signs and still buzzes the phone. Reset with `npx convex run seed:run`. Never show the Convex data browser: it lists per-worker rows.

**If the Value card, Playbooks or voice mode aren't live:** Say those beats over the open map, read the numbers from the stats sheet, and type instead of talking.

**If the numbers look odd:** Read them off the screen. Any text adds the 5th voice, only a work task adds hours, and PLAN needs one first. "2 hours a week" gives 21 hrs/week and $13,104 a quarter.
