# ezworkspace: pitch package

## 1. One-liner and taglines

**One-liner:** ezworkspace is a private iMessage coach that gets workers to share the AI shortcuts they hide, because their COO sees the team's redesign map only after guaranteeing that no worker ends up worse off.

1. AI savings, shared with the people who made them.
2. Better off, not laid off.
3. We don't get paid until your workers do.

## 4. Ten hardest questions

1. **How big is this?** Every back-office worker in a non-tech industry is a $6-a-month seat, and the median company already spends about $11–12 per employee per month on AI ([BERI](https://www.beri.net/article/ai-spending-7500-per-employee-ramp-index-2026)). We'll size the TAM bottom-up, not guess.
2. **Why now?** 57% hide their AI use; only 40% have an AI policy ([HRGrapevine](https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-05-02-covert-use-of-artificial-intelligence-at-work-poses-risk-study-finds)). Workday entered the category Oct 13 ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-13-Workday-Introduces-Work-Intelligence-to-Help-Customers-Design-Work-for-AI)); Colorado's AI disclosure law starts Jan 1, 2027 ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/payroll/overhaul-of-colorado-ai-bias-law-headed-to-polis-for-signature)).
3. **Moat vs Reejig and Workday?** They sell to the employer, so workers won't tell them what they hide, and they can't credibly seal data from their own customer. Reejig fits 10,000+ employee firms ([RFP.wiki](https://www.rfp.wiki/enterprise-software-enterprise-application-software-eas-enterprise-service-management-esm/talent-intelligence-platforms/reejig)); Workday's tool is early access for its own HCM customers ([Workday](https://newsroom.workday.com/2026-10-13-Workday-Introduces-Work-Intelligence-to-Help-Customers-Design-Work-for-AI)).
4. **Why would a COO pay for something workers control?** In the demo, Acme keeps 67% of savings it couldn't see before signing. Klarna's AI took two-thirds of its chats, then its CEO said quality suffered and it began hiring humans again ([Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/klarna-ceo-reverses-course-by-hiring-more-humans-not-ai/491396)).
5. **Isn't this a nicer layoff tool?** No map until the employer signs "no pay cuts or AI layoffs for 12 months"; team totals only; Firecrawl flags any signer's WARN notice for human review. We charge per employee and on dividends paid, so layoffs cut our revenue.
6. **Unit economics?** As hypotheses: a 100-person department pays $12k, then $7.2k a year, plus 10% on top of dividends. That's below the $50–100K proof-of-concept benchmark ([coworker.ai](https://coworker.ai/blog/how-much-should-enterprise-ai-cost-per-user)) and inside HR AI's $5–15 per employee per month ([Workativ](https://workativ.com/hr/blog/hr-chatbot-pricing)).
7. **What if workers lie?** Self-reports only draw the map; pilot dividends pay on measured team cycle time against a baseline, so inflating minutes earns nothing. Tonight's demo uses reported minutes.
8. **Is the Compact enforceable?** It's a pilot-contract clause backed by the 20% escrow and a public badge the company can lose; no-layoff terms already appear in union AI contracts ([metaintro](https://www.metaintro.com/blog/unions-ai-protections-2026-contracts), a weaker source). Counsel reviews it before the first paid pilot.
9. **Go-to-market?** Top-down: a COO with a productivity target buys one pilot, starting in AP, where cycle time is measurable. Then bottom-up: workers recruit teammates to reach quorum and share dividend receipts. iMessage today; SMS and web next.
10. **Why you?** I'm in SJSU's Responsible Computing Club, and making AI adoption fair to workers is the problem I chose. I built this loop tonight and talked to [N] workers while doing it.

## 6. Demo-failure playbook

**Before 9:00:** Record a 90-second backup video and a Nebula screenshot. Join the iPhone hotspot once. Sign in as the COO in two browser profiles; rehearse the commands below. The on-screen Compact says only "No affected worker's pay is cut for 12 months": fix it, or say what's on screen.

**Before every judge:** Double-click Reset demo (back to 4/5, unsigned). Phone on the ezworkspace thread with Focus off; relay running (`bun start` in `agent/`).

**If iMessage lags:** Say "iMessage is taking a second" and keep explaining the Compact. If the counter hit 5/5, the text landed: sign anyway, and the receipt catches up. Nothing after 20 seconds? Say "Same backend, from the laptop," then in `web/`, with `AGENT_SECRET` exported:

```sh
npx convex run ingest:text "{\"secret\":\"$AGENT_SECRET\",\"from\":\"+1YOURCELL\",\"body\":\"ChatGPT drafts my vendor emails, saves me 2 hours a week\"}"
```

The reply prints and the counter flips; rerun with `PLAN` as the body. If the relay crashed, `bun start` again; queued receipts still go out.

**If Wi-Fi drops:** Move the laptop to the iPhone hotspot; the phone's iMessage uses cellular. No signal at all: play the video, show the screenshot, say "The network's down; this is the same flow, recorded tonight," then Q&A.

**If sign-in fails:** Switch to the second profile. If Clerk is down, `npx convex run team:signCompact --identity '{"name":"COO, Acme Insurance","subject":"coo","issuer":"cli"}'` signs and still buzzes the phone; the video shows the dashboard. Reset with `npx convex run seed:run`. Never open the Convex data browser in front of a judge: it lists per-worker rows.

**If the numbers look odd:** Read them off the screen. Any text adds the 5th voice, only a work task adds hours, and PLAN needs one first. "2 hours a week" gives $13,104: $3,931 to workers, $393 fee, $8,780 to Acme.
